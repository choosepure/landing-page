What is TBHQ (Tert-Butylhydroquinone)? It is a petroleum-derived synthetic antioxidant added to cooking oils to prevent them from going rancid and extend shelf life. The EU banned it entirely in 2004 after studies raised concerns about its genotoxicity (DNA damage), potential carcinogenicity at high doses, and immune system effects. India and the US still permit it up to 200 ppm.

Should parents be concerned? At 190.20 ppm — just 4.9% below the India/US maximum — this is not a violation of Indian law. However, regular daily cooking with an oil carrying near-maximum TBHQ levels means continuous low-level exposure, which is what the EU's cautionary stance is based on. The EWG gives TBHQ a concern rating of 3/10 (moderate) based on immune system and behaviour studies. Families who cook daily with this oil (as most Indian households do) may want to consider this.
